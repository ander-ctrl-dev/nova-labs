How Computers Think

Have you ever wondered how electricity becomes the digital information we use every day across the world? Text messages, video games, websites, mobile apps, and sound all begin as either 1s or 0s, known as binary code. Binary is the language computers speak. It all starts with electricity flowing from the power supply into tiny electronic circuits called logic gates. A computer is really just millions — or billions — of tiny switches turning on and off extremely quickly.

It all starts with simple ON and OFF states.

1. Electricity and Switches

ON
OFF

Imagine a light switch.

When the switch is ON, electricity flows.

When the switch is OFF, electricity stops.

ON = electricity flowing
OFF = no electricity

2. Understanding Binary

1

Electricity flowing = 1

0

No electricity = 0

Computers only understand two electrical states: ON and OFF.

Humans use numbers like: 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5...

Computers use binary instead: 0 and 1.

Each 0 or 1 is called a bit. Groups of bits (8 bits is called a byte) can represent letters, numbers, colors, sounds, and instructions.

01001000 = H
01101001 = i

When combined together:

01001000 01101001 = "Hi"

Hexadecimal

Binary is useful for computers, but long binary numbers are difficult for humans to read.

To make things easier, programmers often use hexadecimal instead.

Hexadecimal uses: 0-9 and A-F to represent larger binary values in a shorter form.

Binary: 11111111
Hexadecimal: FF

Hexadecimal is commonly used in programming, memory addresses, and color values.

3. Transistors

Modern computers contain billions of microscopic switches called transistors.

A transistor can either allow electricity to flow, or block it. This creates ON and OFF states.

One transistor by itself is simple. But billions of transistors working together can run games, websites, operating systems, and artificial intelligence.

A transistor acts like a tiny electronic switch.

ON = 1 OFF = 0

4. Logic Gates

Logic gates are tiny decision-making circuits. They take electrical inputs and produce outputs based on simple rules.

Computers combine millions of logic gates together to process information and make decisions.

AND Gate

An AND gate only turns ON if BOTH inputs are ON.

Example: A phone may require both a correct password AND a fingerprint to unlock.

OR Gate

An OR gate turns ON if at least one input is ON.

Example: You can log in with a username OR an email address.

NOT Gate

A NOT gate reverses the signal.

If the input is ON, the output becomes OFF.

5. How Information Exists

Computers convert everything into binary: letters, numbers, images, music, and videos.

Each piece of information is stored using combinations of electrical ON and OFF signals.

A → 65 → 01000001

Even the words on this page are ultimately stored as patterns of electricity.
